### 💡 Why this protocol ?

Even when using a tool like https://github.github.com/spec-kit/, you get only one model's view on your problem, and even with a secondary model review, your spec might still have important gaps, design flaws, that you or your models might have missed.

This is not speculative, this is what I could observe while I was refining a spec through a simple prompt (at the time), where I asked for a few models to review the spec. Each came up with gaps, flaws, suggested improvements, warnings, etc.

I built this to catch the edge cases we miss when writing specs, and to help catch issues early, before the implementation phase! See [this presentation on the Consensus Review Protocol](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1hm-TmbDlXiyOKYeEbIw32b2dvCoA7LASwfthi1S65Pg/edit?slide=id.g3df0bc42be9_2_131#slide=id.g3df0bc42be9_2_131) I did to the team for more details.

### 🛠️ How it works

You feed it a technical spec or design document, and it runs (by default) three AI models (GPT 5.6 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5, and Claude Opus 5) through a three-phase review.

* :blue_circle: **Blue Team:** Acts as a structural advocate. It proves your spec can be executed as written, checks for internal contradictions, and lists the load-bearing assumptions.
* :red_circle: **Red Team:** Attacks the spec. It uses the Blue team's assumptions as a target list to hunt for fundamental flaws, missing edge cases, and compound risks, and also looks further for flaws blues had missed.
* :purple_circle: **Purple Team:** Acts as the judge and chaos-engineering team. It stress-tests the spec with chaos engineering scenarios and arbitrates the Red team's attacks to determine what actually needs fixing.

Severity changes between phases require majority, full dismissal requires unanimous agreement, and Red/Purple are structurally required to independently re-examine Blue's findings rather than rubber-stamp them.

A **Proportionality** principle is embedded into the models review modes to signal if raised items are anticipated or demonstrated, to make sure the Tech Lead makes an informed choice when accepting or rejecting a change.

### 🛡️ Why it is safe to rely on

* **No AI guessing:** The core rule of the entire protocol is *NEVER GUESS*. If the AI is unsure about a requirement or an edit, it stops and asks you rather than making a silent assumption.
* **You are in control:** The models do not decide what is sound, and they never edit your spec behind your back. They generate a `resolutions` file where you explicitly `ACCEPT`, `REJECT`, or `MODIFY` every single finding.
* **Deterministic:** We consolidated the protocol so it relies on strict, mechanical rules so it won't hallucinate or silently retry on a failure (it stops and asks you instead).

### 🤑 A fair warning on token cost

Please keep an eye on your token spending when using this skill!

By default, the skill runs your spec through three medium-to-expensive models (though you can configure this at the prompt). Because it runs sequentially, each phase has to read your spec *plus* all the outputs generated by the previous phases. On large specs, this context grows very fast.

To help manage this, the skill will give you an advisory warning if your spec alone is over 10,000 words, and it enforces a hard stop (asking for permission to continue) in your Copilot session if the total context reaches 80,000 words in any phase.

In order to reduce costs, there's also:

* An **Anti-Echo-Chamber** discipline embedded in the skill to prevent red and purple teams to plainly restate their predecessors.
* An **Economy of Expression** principle to reduce wordiness of the LLMs

I recommend running the skill with Claude Sonnet (or cheaper models) as Orchestrator as the last phase run by it is the most expensive. While blue / red / purple models might need high-tier models with higher reasoning abilities, the Orchestrator can likely settle with medium-tier models with large context windows. Low-tier models might give wrong results though as it is the Orchestrator who's tasked to implement the approved changes.

### How to use

In order to use it, just issue this prompt: `please run a consensus review of <spec-file>` and follow the session prompts / reports. You can specify the number of models and their names in the prompt, on each round of course.

It also works on plans, specs, copilot skills, documents you want hardened.

When the first round has completed, the skill will present you a **Completion Report**, inviting you to check the `consolidated` report and the `resolutions` file where you will be able to review raised items and decide on a resolution for each of them, review the questions raised by the models during the review.

When you're ready to go for another round, just write `please go for another round` in the same session.

### When to stop

When you are satisfied with the previous run and consider the spec is sound / ready for being acted upon, you'll need to prompt the orchestrator to `go for a final round`. This will skip blue/red/purple phases and apply the last resolutions from previous round. Please note if you modify any proposed fix, your amendments won't have been cross-reviewed, so it's better to do a final round on an accept/reject-only round (or accept the limitation of that final round).

It's easy to over-engineer, I've done that myself, by going too deep in the review, make sure to carefully review LLMs suggestions before accepting them, and know when to stop!
